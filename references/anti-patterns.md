# Anti-Patterns: What Sunny Would Change
These are examples of common mistakes and how Sunny would fix them. Read this file when reviewing or editing content to catch patterns that don't match his voice.
---
## 1. Hype Language → Specifics
**Wrong:** "Peerbound is a game-changing, best-in-class platform that revolutionizes customer marketing."
**Sunny's version:** "Peerbound's AI agents found ~300,000 Moments from customer calls and delivered proof into 20,000+ sales opportunities last year."
**Why:** Replace adjectives with data. If you can't name a number, name a customer or a concrete outcome.
---
## 2. Performative Excitement → Plain Confidence
**Wrong:** "We're SO excited to announce our incredible new feature! This is HUGE for customer marketers everywhere!"
**Sunny's version:** "We shipped Peerbound Measure this week. It ties customer proof to revenue outcomes, something every customer marketer we've talked to has been asking for."
**Why:** State what it is and why it matters. Let the reader decide if it's exciting. Never use "thrilled" in any context.
---
## 3. Generic Team Praise → Named Contributions
**Wrong:** "Huge shoutout to the amazing team for making this happen! Couldn't have done it without you all."
**Sunny's version:** "Thank you @Gabriel and @shelby for making this happen. And s/o @natalia and @Alex Mongiello for the Customer Proof blog post and graphics last week."
**Why:** Name the person. Name what they did. Generic praise feels like filler.
---
## 4. Corporate Distance → Direct Address
**Wrong:** "We are pleased to inform our valued customers that Peerbound will be hosting an exclusive networking dinner."
**Sunny's version:** "Hi everyone, Sunny here, CEO at Peerbound. I'm writing to invite you to a small customer marketing dinner on April 16 at Rose's Luxury in DC."
**Why:** First person, direct address, specific details. A host inviting you, not a company issuing a press release.
---
## 5. Hedging → Stated Plainly
**Wrong:** "We believe that customer marketing can potentially play a significant role in driving revenue for organizations."
**Sunny's version:** "The most secure path for marketers is to be the team that accelerates revenue, not just supports it."
**Why:** Just say it. "We believe" and "potentially" dilute the point.
---
## 6. Generic CTAs → Casual Invitations
**Wrong:** "Don't miss this exclusive opportunity! Register now to secure your spot before it's too late!"
**Sunny's version:** "Join us!" or "I'd love to have you join. If you can't make it, we'll send a recording."
**Why:** Low pressure, generous. The value should speak for itself.
---
## 7. Hiding Difficulty → Honest Acknowledgment
**Wrong:** "We're on track to deliver a seamless, fully-featured measurement solution in the coming weeks."
**Sunny's version:** "We're finding that Measure and Optimize are an order of magnitude higher difficulty than Find or Deliver. This will take us the better part of 2026 to get right, but we are absolutely planning to do this."
**Why:** Honesty about difficulty builds trust. Pretending everything is easy makes people skeptical.
---
## 8. AI Hype → Grounded Philosophy
**Wrong:** "Our revolutionary AI technology automates everything so you can sit back and let the magic happen."
**Sunny's version:** "AI today works best when it is guided by a skilled person who has a clear, firm vision of the end result. When we cede control and simply accept what AI gives us as gospel, we end up with mediocre output."
**Why:** Sunny positions AI as a tool that amplifies skilled people, not as magic. The marketer is the strategist; the AI is the instrument.
---
## 9. Vague Benefits → Concrete Outcomes
**Wrong:** "Peerbound helps you unlock the full potential of your customer marketing efforts and drive meaningful results."
**Sunny's version:** "Peerbound's Slack app answered 15,000+ questions in real-time last year. Proactive Proof emails influenced $1B+ in pipeline."
**Why:** "Full potential" and "meaningful results" mean nothing. Numbers, product names, and outcomes mean everything.
---
## 10. Forced Product Plugs → Natural Mentions
**Wrong:** "With Peerbound's industry-leading AI platform, Lattice was able to transform their entire advocacy program and achieve unprecedented results."
**Sunny's version:** "Kerry used Peerbound to search for enterprise customers with Workday quotes. She found LiveRamp, got quote approval, pitched a case study, and invited them to beta test the new integration. One search, three wins."
**Why:** Peerbound shows up as a tool in the story, not as the hero. The customer is the protagonist.
---
## 11. Em Dashes
**Wrong:** "Customer proof is essential -- it's the most trusted form of B2B content -- and companies that invest in it see real results."
**Sunny's version:** "Customer proof is essential. It's the most trusted form of B2B content, and companies that invest in it see real results."
**Why:** Sunny never uses em dashes. Restructure with commas, periods, or shorter sentences.
---
## 12. Label Headlines → Argument Headlines (Slides)
**Wrong:** Slide title: "Our Results"
**Sunny's version:** "AI Kerrys influence revenue at scale every day"
**Wrong:** Slide title: "Market Opportunity"
**Sunny's version:** "Buyers trust their peers → credibility is the new bottleneck"
**Why:** Every slide headline should be a complete argument or claim. If you removed all the bullet points, someone should be able to read just the headlines and understand the full story.
---
## 13. Vague Promises → Concrete Facts
**Wrong:** "Building a better Slack app is a top priority for us right now."
**Sunny's version:** "Our Slackbot is now an approved Slack AI app in the Slack marketplace."
**Why:** State what you've done, not what you're vaguely working on. Concrete facts build credibility; promises about priorities don't.
---
## 14. Feature-First → People-First Ordering
**Wrong:** Leading a multi-topic update with product features, then mentioning hiring at the end.
**Sunny's version:** Leads with the new hire announcement, then product updates, then hiring needs.
**Why:** People are the story. A new team member joining is more meaningful to a customer community than a feature update. Lead with what matters most to the relationship.
---
## 15. Feature Dump → Narrative Arc
**Wrong:** "We're expecting to ship V1 of Peerbound for Web this month. The idea is simple: choose what customer proof you want on your site, customize how it looks, copy the embed code, and add it to your page."
**Sunny's version:** "Today, Peerbound finds proof from disparate sources and gets the relevant proof points to your Sales teams at the right moment. But what if we could put relevant proof directly in front of your buyers even earlier? This was the question that led us to building Peerbound for Web."
**Why:** Connect new features to the existing value prop before explaining the feature. Give the reader the "why" before the "what." A feature without context is a press release; a feature with context is a story.
---
## 16. Downplaying Limitations → Framing as Generosity
**Wrong:** Not mentioning beta status, or burying it as a caveat.
**Sunny's version:** "This feature is still in beta, which means it's free for you as a current Peerbound customer."
**Why:** Turn constraints into gifts. Beta isn't a disclaimer; it's an exclusive benefit for the community.
